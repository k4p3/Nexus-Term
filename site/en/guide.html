Usage guide

From the first launch to your daily workflow. Press Ctrl+Shift+/ in the app at any time to see every shortcut.

  1. Create your vault

    On first launch NexusTerm asks for a master password. It protects every credential and key you store. Choose a long one: it cannot be recovered.

    • NexusTerm opens a local terminal at launch and only asks for the password when you use a saved server. If you prefer it to ask at startup, turn that on in Settings.
    • Lock the vault with the button in the top bar, or let it lock itself after a while without activity (10 minutes by default, configurable in Settings). Your open sessions keep working; only new connections are blocked until you unlock.
    • Change the master password later from Settings; your servers are not re-encrypted.

  2. Add a server

    In the left sidebar create folders (for example Production or Staging), then add a host with its address, port and identity.

    • Identities are reusable credentials (password or private key) that you can assign to many servers.
    • Generate an Ed25519 or RSA key pair inside the app and copy the public key to ~/.ssh/authorized_keys.
    • Pick a jump host to reach servers behind a bastion.
    • Choose the protocol: SSH, RDP or VNC.

  3. Connect

    Double-click a host, or press Ctrl+K for Quick Connect and type part of its name. The first time you connect, NexusTerm shows the server's fingerprint so you can trust it (like known_hosts). You can also open a local terminal with the + button or Ctrl+Shift+T. Press Ctrl+Space for command suggestions from your history and snippets. Switch tabs with Ctrl+Tab, or jump straight to one with Alt+1…9.

  4. Work with several panes

    • Ctrl+Shift+O splits horizontally and Ctrl+Shift+E vertically; each new pane can be a local shell, the same host or another one.
    • Ctrl+Shift+W closes the active pane.
    • Broadcast (Ctrl+Shift+B) types in every pane at once: handy for updating a cluster. A banner warns you while it is on.

  5. Transfer files with SFTP

    Use the SFTP button in the tab bar to open the explorer for the active host. Drag and drop or select several files; transfers run in a queue with progress indicators, and you can set how many run at the same time in Settings.

  6. Open tunnels

    Open the port forwarding manager to create local (-L), remote (-R) or dynamic SOCKS5 (-D) tunnels for a host, and start or stop them whenever you need.

  7. Reuse commands with snippets

    The snippet drawer keeps your frequent commands. Use {{placeholders}} and NexusTerm asks for the values and then types the command at your prompt without running it, so you review it and press Enter. A catalogue of community snippets for Docker, systemd, network and storage is included, and you can clone them to customise.

    Press Ctrl+Shift+P to search every terminal action, theme and snippet.

  8. Share snippets

    Use Export and Import in the snippet drawer to exchange snippets as a JSON file. Importing shows every command for review, flags risky ones, skips duplicates and never runs anything. Only import files you trust.

  9. Accept inline suggestions

    While you type, the likely continuation appears in gray after the cursor, taken from your history and snippets. Press → or End to accept it, or Ctrl+→ for one word. Nothing runs until you press Enter, and you can turn it off in Settings.

  10. Use command blocks

    With shell integration (OSC 133, supported in Zsh, Bash and Fish; NexusTerm enables it automatically in local terminals, and you can turn it off in Settings) each command becomes a block with a ✓/✗ mark and its duration. Click the mark to select a block, then copy its command or output, search only inside it with Ctrl+F, or re-run it with Ctrl+Shift+R. Use Ctrl+Shift+↑/↓ to jump between prompts.

  11. Find old commands and recover lost output

    • Ctrl+Shift+H opens the history of the current host. Enter inserts a command, Ctrl+Enter runs it. Commands starting with a space or that look like secrets are never recorded.
    • Ctrl+Shift+Y opens Instant Replay, a read-only timeline of the last 5 minutes of output, even after a clear. It lives in memory only.
    • Ctrl+Shift+Space starts Quick Select to copy URLs, paths, hashes or IPs with the keyboard.

  12. Pick up where you left off

    NexusTerm saves the layout of your tabs and panes (hosts and titles only, never contents or credentials) and offers to restore it after you unlock the vault, with a discreet notice that does not block the screen. Tabs reconnect when you first open them. Choose Ask, Always or Never in Settings.

  13. Make it yours

    Open Settings (Ctrl+,) to change the theme, font, language and the privacy options: history, Instant Replay, session restore, remote clipboard (OSC 52), inline images and font ligatures.

    Font ligatures need a ligature font installed on your system, such as JetBrains Mono or Fira Code. Settings tells you if the font in use does not have them.
About RDP and VNC. VNC is complete. RDP supports video, keyboard, mouse and certificate trust on first use; its text clipboard and dynamic resize are still in development.
